**D1 (methodology_rigor).** This mandatory dimension belongs to the methodology seat, and I will not score it. Read from the domain side, it asks whether the empirical design would satisfy referees who work on applied on-device and health-sensing ML: data split at the level of the independent unit (the person, not the overlapping window) so that leakage cannot inflate results; preprocessing, calibration-set and threshold choices fixed before any test data are consulted; uncertainty estimated at the participant or cluster level rather than by treating correlated windows as independent draws; many comparisons corrected for, or at least disclosed; pre-specified tests kept apart from after-the-fact exploration; and code, seeds, pinned environments and released artifacts sufficient for an outsider to regenerate every number. Where a domain convention bears on these points, for example how sensor gaps, sensor warm-up periods or a dataset's sampling interval interact with sliding-window construction, I will pass the fact to the methodology seat rather than score it myself.

**D2 (domain_accuracy).** This mandatory dimension is the one my seat owns. I take it to require that the work be right about both of its parent fields and about the toolchain that connects them. Earlier findings must be restated as their sources actually report them, on the same data, metric and setting; the technical vocabulary of quantization, of on-device inference and of continuous glucose monitoring must carry its established meaning; statements about converters, runtimes and devices must match how those tools are documented to behave at the versions used; and the reference base must engage the work a specialist would expect to see before accepting the contribution as positioned. How much a domain error weighs depends on what rests on it: a slip that no conclusion depends on is minor, while an error beneath a reported result or the central claim can decide the outcome.

**D3 (argumentative_coherence).** This mandatory dimension is owned by the devil's-advocate seat, with the methodology seat also eligible. I read it as asking whether the central thesis survives its own evidence: headline claims should not outrun the results, sections should not contradict one another, and the reasoning should avoid moves such as treating a non-significant difference as demonstrated equivalence, extending a finding from one benchmark population to deployment in general, or reading parity on a single aggregate metric as proof that a model change carries no trade-off. On the domain side, the overreach typical of this field is a drift from a research-prototype result toward implied clinical usefulness, and I will report any domain overstatement of that kind to the owning seat.

**D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance).** This high-priority dimension belongs to the perspective seat. It concerns whether neighboring communities can use the work: an embedded-ML engineer should be able to follow the glucose-monitoring framing, and a diabetes-technology or clinical-informatics reader should be able to follow the quantization and deployment framing. Terms that mean different things in these communities should be defined on first use, and any claim about implications for another discipline should be supported rather than asserted. My part is to flag terms whose meaning shifts between these communities, so the owning seat can judge whether they are adequately defined.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each domain statement that a result or conclusion rests on is correct and current, across four strands. (1) Prior work: cited findings restated with the source's own dataset, split, horizon or label definition, metric and precision/runtime setting, and every "first" or novelty claim tested against the CGM forecasting, trend-arrow, on-device health-sensing and quantization literature. (2) Terminology: post-training quantization vs quantization-aware training; dynamic-range vs full-integer vs float16 schemes; weight-only vs weight-and-activation INT8; per-tensor vs per-channel and symmetric vs asymmetric scales; "calibration" kept distinct across its three senses in this field (PTQ activation-range estimation on a representative set, probabilistic calibration of classifier outputs, CGM sensor calibration against reference blood glucose); resampling terms matched to what the cited method actually resamples (participant-level cluster bootstrap vs window-level or block bootstrap); and CGM facts, namely device- and dataset-specific sampling intervals, rate-of-change units (mg/dL/min vs mg/dL per sample vs mmol/L/min), trend-arrow bands attributed to the manufacturer they come from (bands differ between manufacturers), CGM-reported interstitial values with their physiological lag and sensor error kept distinct from blood glucose, and consensus glycemic ranges cited to their source. (3) Toolchain and device claims: converter and runtime names and versions (TensorFlow Lite, now LiteRT; Core ML; ONNX Runtime; ExecuTorch), delegate or backend choice, partial delegation or fallback of unsupported ops, and float vs integer model I/O, all consistent with the tool's documentation, with latency, size or energy figures tied to a named device, OS, runtime version, backend, thread count and warm-up protocol rather than inferred from emulators, simulators, desktop hosts or the nominal 4x float32-to-INT8 ratio. (4) Coverage: the anchors a specialist expects engaged (integer-only inference and PTQ white papers or surveys, MLPerf Tiny/Mobile-style on-device benchmarking, public CGM datasets and benchmarks, deep time-series-classification baselines, ML leakage and reproducibility work, cluster-level resampling methods), with clinical or regulatory-status wording (medical device, SaMD, clinical decision support) either absent or used in its formal sense.
what_triggers_block: A domain error or omission that changes what a reported result, comparison or contribution claim means but that revision can correct without invalidating the core result: a prior finding misquoted, or compared across mismatched data, split, label definition, metric or bit-width, so that a comparison or novelty claim no longer holds as written; uncited prior work on the same task or data that contradicts a "first" claim while a distinct contribution still stands; one quantization scheme or one sense of "calibration" presented as another so that evidence for one is offered for the other (dynamic-range quantization described as full-integer INT8, probability-calibration evidence offered for PTQ calibration); a CGM unit, rate-unit or threshold-source misstatement in the text that changes how the stated classes or results must be read; a converter or runtime behavior claim contradicted by that tool's documentation where a headline efficiency figure depends on it, or emulator, simulator or desktop timing reported as on-device latency; or explicit wording outside the central claim that implies clinical validity, regulatory status or treatment guidance.
what_triggers_warn: Isolated domain imprecision that a specialist would correct but that leaves every conclusion and comparison intact: loose or inconsistent quantization or CGM terminology that context resolves; a secondary citation misattributed, or a quoted prior number slightly off, without changing any comparison; outdated toolchain naming, or a missing runtime version, backend, thread count or warm-up detail for a non-headline measurement; trend-arrow bands or glycemic ranges stated without their source; ambiguous phrasing that a reader could take as a clinical implication where no such claim is made; or an expected literature anchor (a standard baseline family, survey or benchmark) missing in a way that thins context without changing how the contribution is positioned.
what_triggers_fatal: The central contribution or headline result rests on a domain-factual error that no revision can repair without changing the contribution itself: the artifact behind the central result is not what is claimed (for example, figures reported for a quantized model were produced by its float counterpart, or the claimed configuration is one the named toolchain cannot produce or execute), so the central comparison has no valid evidence; a defining domain quantity (unit, sampling interval, rate threshold or label definition) is wrong inside the pipeline itself, so every reported result concerns a different task from the one claimed; the core finding is presented as new although prior work already reported it for the same task and data, leaving no distinct contribution; or the main claim is clinical validity or treatment guidance that the evidence cannot support.
