## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): The study's design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility affordances must clear the peer-review bar of empirical applied ML. From the editorial chair I read this as the floor everything else rests on. Under the contract's field notes that means confirmatory analyses kept apart from exploratory ones, leakage controlled, uncertainty estimated in a way that respects clustered and dependent data, multiplicity handled, and artifacts that let an outside reader rerun the work. I do not score this dimension, but a block here sets how much weight I can put on any contribution claim.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): Claims must match current domain evidence, prior work must be represented fairly, and domain terminology and reported results must be free of factual error. Editorially, this decides whether the paper's account of its own novelty can be trusted. A contribution framed against a misdescribed baseline or literature is weaker than it looks, so I will take the domain seat's findings into account without scoring them myself.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate seat with methodology eligible): The core thesis must be internally consistent, the evidence must actually carry the claims made from it, and no fallacy may undercut the central argument. For editorial oversight this is the test of whether the headline message follows from what was measured, especially when a careful study's conclusions reach further than its design allows.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by the perspective seat): Framing, definitions and implications must be accessible to readers in adjacent fields, and any claim that crosses discipline lines must be substantiated. Editorially, this bears on readership breadth. A narrow empirical study earns a general ML-evaluation audience only if readers outside the application area can see what to carry back to their own practice.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by me): The manuscript's organisation, clarity of exposition, figure and table quality, and adherence to the conventions of its venue class. At the stated length this covers whether the argument can be navigated, whether the main text and supporting material are divided sensibly, and whether figures and tables can be read on their own. With no venue configured, I judge conventions against general archival ML-methodology norms rather than any one venue's template.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by me): The manuscript must suit its intended readership and make an original, significant contribution that readership would value. No target venue is configured, so I read "fit" as fit to a field-general empirical-ML-methodology readership with digital-health ML as a cross-listed audience. The test is whether the work offers a reusable lesson, protocol or negative result that would change how practitioners evaluate or deploy models, not only a correct account of a single system. Because this dimension is mandatory, a fatal finding here alone can end the submission.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: A clear path from motivation to contribution statement to evidence to limitations; sections sized to the argument rather than to the history of the work; the boundary between main-text claims and supplementary material stated explicitly; figures and tables that can be read standalone, with units, sample sizes and uncertainty shown; consistent terminology and notation; and numbers that agree across abstract, body, tables, figures and cited result files.
what_triggers_block: The core argument cannot be followed without repeated cross-referencing because of structural sprawl, or headline numbers conflict between the abstract, body, tables or figures in ways that would mislead a reader about the main result.
what_triggers_warn: Presentation problems that slow the reader without misleading them, such as excess length relative to contribution, redundancy across sections, figures or tables missing uncertainty or sample-size annotations, inconsistent terminology, or isolated numeric mismatches that do not affect the headline claims.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: A crisply stated contribution that is distinct from prior work; evidence that the lesson generalises beyond the single system studied, or an honest scoping of where it does not; significance proportionate to the claims, with confirmatory findings separated from descriptive ones; and a takeaway a broad ML-evaluation readership could act on, with digital-health relevance as a secondary audience. Judged against a field-general readership because no venue is configured.
what_triggers_block: The contribution as stated is either not clearly distinct from established practice or rests mainly on post-hoc or descriptive findings presented as confirmatory, so a general ML-methodology readership would not find a practice-changing lesson without substantial reframing or added evidence.
what_triggers_warn: The contribution is real and original but narrower than its framing suggests, its generality is asserted more than demonstrated, or its significance to readers outside the application area is left implicit when it should be stated.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript offers no original contribution to any plausible ML or health-informatics readership, for example because it duplicates existing work without a new finding, or because its central contribution depends on a clinical-validity or treatment claim that falls outside the contract's stated scope.
